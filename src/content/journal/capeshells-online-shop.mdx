<p class="text-prose leading-prose m-5">
  A couple of years ago, Cape Atlantis Shells launched with an identity built to survive wherever people would actually run into it — a business card, social media, the side of a delivery van.
</p>

<p class="text-prose leading-prose m-5">
  The van turned out to be the one that mattered most. The founder told me recently that a good share of his orders trace straight back to it — people seeing the decals roll past and looking the business up. He also mentioned he'd like to wear the brand himself, not just drive it around.
</p>

<p class="text-prose leading-prose m-5">
  So this week, the shop went live. Hoodies, hats, and a few accessories, built on the same identity from the original sketches — sitting in someone's cart instead of on a driveway or a job site.
</p>

<p class="text-prose leading-prose m-5">
  I don't think founders always clock which piece of the brand is doing the real work. Sometimes it's the thing you didn't think to measure — a decal on a van, doing just as much lifting as the website. The founder noticed. So now, we’re trying merch. 
</p>

<p class="text-prose leading-prose m-5">
  A brand isn't finished when the identity is approved. It has to go somewhere people actually are — a product, a website, a storefront, the side of a van somebody sees every day.
</p>

<p class="text-prose leading-prose m-5">
  The Land & Sea Collection is live now.
</p>
