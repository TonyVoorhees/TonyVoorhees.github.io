<p class="font-serif font-350 text-[clamp(1.5rem,2.2vw,2rem)] leading-[1.4] m-0 dropcap tracking-tight">
    I designed my high school yearbook cover. Sold my first website not long after — to a machining company, if you're wondering how glamorous the beginning was. I've been doing creative work for other people ever since, including the years I had a manager, a badge, and a W2. Tony Voorhees Studio isn't a new chapter. It's the first time I'm saying the quiet part out loud.
</p>
<p class="font-serif font-350 text-[clamp(1.5rem,2.2vw,2rem)] leading-[1.4] m-0 dropcap tracking-tight">
  Somewhere along the way, I stopped thinking of myself as someone who does product design or brand work and started noticing they were never really separate. The best ideas don't stop at the edge of a discipline—they carry through when you treat the seams as part of the work. That's mostly what this studio is: paying attention to the places other people skip past.
</p>

<Marginalia text="Today" mt={56}>
  <p class="text-prose leading-prose m-0">
    These days I work mostly with founders and small teams moving fast, where every decision touches the next one, and there's no time to wait for three different people to weigh in. I like it there. It's the same instinct I've had since the yearbook cover, just with better tools and slightly higher stakes.
  </p>
</Marginalia>
