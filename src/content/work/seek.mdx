  <div class="mx-auto prose max-w-(--container-prose)">
    <h2>The Challenge</h2>
    <p>Seek AI 1.0 asked business users to write SQL to get answers from their own data. It was powerful, but the experience assumed technical fluency.</p>
    <p class="font-serif italic font-light text-2xl"><span class="font-mono text-accent"> → </span> A tool built for engineers, sold to executives.</p>
    <p>Users struggled to get started, verify AI-generated answers, and work with their teams.</p>
    <p>Seek needed to become a product a much broader audience could use — and I had four months to get there.</p>
  </div>
</section>

  <div class="mx-auto prose max-w-(--container-prose)">
    <h2>The Insight</h2>
    <p><b>Users had already solved the interface problem.</b></p>
    <p>In interviews, I kept hearing the same workaround: ask ChatGPT to write the SQL, paste it into Seek, and hope it runs. They weren't looking for another tool to operate. They wanted to ask a question and get an answer.</p>
    <p class="font-serif italic font-light text-2xl"><span class="font-mono text-accent"> → </span>Seek needed to be conversational, not transactional.</p>
    <p><b>A person you ask, not a tool you operate.</b> That became the foundation for the redesign.</p>
  </div>
</section>
